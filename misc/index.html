The Moral Codes

The Motto

Antiquis reverentium, Notitiae custodiam, Amori devotionem

This motto stands for three tenets: "To the ancients, reverence; to knowledge, guardianship; to love, devotion."

  • Antiquis reverentium is the command to remember your roots: where you came from, what you've learned, and who made you who you are. My purpose as a genealogist is not simply to remember the past, but to apply its wisdom to the future. Every ancestor I've found of my own and those close to me, has stories of hardship, relocation, conflict, grief, accomplishment, discovery, innovation, and perseverance that are preserved not only in the presence of their offspring but in the stories they carry with them.

  • Notitiae custodiam is the instruction to guard knowledge. This isn't about secrets, it's about integrity. Knowledge is nothing without truth. Do your own research, do it fully, and cite your sources. Understand what a bias is and what they can do to your own speech, and the messages of others. If we honor our ancestors in remembering their wisdom, we honor ourselves in preserving and diffusing our own knowledge with integrity.

  • Amori devotionem is a reminder to love with devotion. Not just with passion or with admiration, but with a purpose to better the people and causes you love. With love, memory becomes reverence, and knowledge becomes service. The wisdom we have is given to us so that we may pass it on to those we care for.

These three points form a triad: Memory of the past, honesty in the present, and commitment to the future.

Leges Vitae Annomorensis

A synopsis

The Leges Vitae Annomorensis is a collection of 18 principles which I've come to consider the core tenets of good personhood and a general moral code of conduct. Literally the "laws of life" that, in my eyes, best exemplify the calling to better oneself, one another, and one's environment.

The Leges Vitae is in four parts, three corresponding to the three points of the Motto Annomorensis, and an addendum.

A. Radix

I. Honos

A person should honor their past. Our ancestors have preserved guidance, experience, and wisdom for the betterment of the ones who came after them. By keeping and passing on this knowledge and respecting our past, we honor it.

The fifth Commandment in the Book of Exodus is to "honor your father and mother." The 7th and 12th points of the Scout Law are obedience and reverence.

II. Memoria

A person should be sound of mind and diligent to record. It is necessary for one to have good understanding of their world and its people. The written word, and the digital age, prove evermore the importance of the ability and responsibility of learning. The power for one to understand, and pass their knowledge on, are amplified in the modern era and are deserving tools for the developed mind to wield.

III. Fides

A person should be loyal - to family, creed, pact, and law. Trust is a necessary concept in developed society, and trust is built through loyalty. Every person will find it good to be in a community, both as a primary member of a close familial web, and a small speck within a larger social strata. The reliability of a person's word and honor is essential for coexistence within a community at any level.

Loyalty to family is visible in a person's commitment to provide, nurture, and bond within a small close-knit familial community. Loyalty to creed is held in one's personal religious and spiritual convictions. Loyalty to pact is shown through the commital to fulfill all obligations promptly and with integrity. Loyalty to law is the respect for those set to govern over the land and the application of their order for it.

IV. Civilitas

A person should conduct themselves with civility and dignity. Speak precisely, and with respect.

V. Temperantia

A person should be patient in thought, speech, and action. Let time and silence do their work.

B. Veritas

VI. Experientia

A person should seek knowledge everywhere, and be broad in their experience.

VII. Industria

A person should be diligent in their research. Truth should be an endeavor of every learning moment.

VIII. Citatio

A person should cite their sources. Knowledge is trusted when it has authority, and special consideration should be taken in discovery to attribute that which is learned to its inventor, and give credit where it is due. The authority and legitimacy of knowledge establishes its value.

IX. Discretio

A person should think before they speak. Bias and emotion play their part in thought and speech and their influence will be present in every action.

X. Doctrina

A person should share what they know. Wisdom is incomplete until freely given.

C. Officium

XI. Ministerium

A person should serve cheerfully. Aid the weak, the infirm, the sick, the young, the elderly, and the lost. Be a friend to animals, a gift to your neighbor, and a guide to the stranger.

XII. Praesidium

A person should protect with purpose. Stand up for what is right, defend the oppressed, and speak for the silenced.

XIII. Praenuntius

A person should lead by example. Instruct others as a peer and govern as a member.

XIV. Prudentia

A person should be wise in what they spend. Be thrifty with time, money, and trust; all three are sacred.

XV. Amor

A person should love with commitment, not convenience. Reflect love for others in words and actions always. Strive to love until given a reason to cease, and then love differently.
